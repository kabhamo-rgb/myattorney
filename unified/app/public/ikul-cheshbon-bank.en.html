Bank Account Garnishment — What's Allowed, What's Protected, and How to Cancel It

A garnishment on your bank account can paralyze your ability to withdraw money. But not all of the money is exposed — the law protects certain amounts and certain types of funds. Here's what you need to know.

In short: Some of the funds in the account are protected from garnishment. If protected funds were garnished from you — you may be entitled to have the garnishment canceled and get a refund.

What is a bank account garnishment?

A bank account garnishment is a procedure in which a creditor, through the Enforcement and Collection Authority or a competent authority, orders the bank to freeze funds in the debtor's account in order to collect a debt. The bank transfers the garnished funds subject to the limits set by law.

Which funds are protected from garnishment?

  • Basic protected amount — there is a minimum amount in the account that may not be garnished, in order to ensure a basic standard of living. This amount is updated from time to time.
  • Benefits and allowances — certain benefits from the National Insurance Institute are protected, in whole or in part.
  • Protected wages — the protected portion of wages deposited into the account continues to enjoy protection.

Garnishment of a joint account

In a joint account, usually the full balance cannot be garnished over the debt of just one of the account holders. The non-debtor spouse may be entitled to protect their share of the funds.

How do you cancel an unjustified garnishment and get a refund?

  1. Obtain an account statement and documents proving the source of the funds (benefit/wages).
  2. Check whether protected funds or an amount exceeding the debt were garnished.
  3. File the appropriate application (for example, a claim of payment ("pareti") or an application to cancel/reduce the garnishment) to recover the funds and stop the collection.
