Incorrect Interest and Linkage in Enforcement — Over-Collection Check

A debt that started at a certain amount can balloon significantly over the years due to interest and linkage. Part of that growth is lawful — but an incorrect calculation or interest beyond what is permitted can inflate the debt unlawfully and entitle you to a refund.

In short: Interest and linkage are permitted within the limits of the law. If the interest was calculated incorrectly, doubled, or exceeded the cap — you may have been over-charged, and a correction and refund can be requested.

How does a debt "balloon" in enforcement?

Arrears interest and index linkage are added to the adjudicated debt, along with expenses and fees. Over time, especially in old files, the total amount may be far higher than the original principal. It is important to verify that every component was calculated lawfully.

When might the calculation be wrong?

  • Interest rate above the cap — arrears interest higher than permitted by law.
  • Double interest — linkage and interest calculated in a way that doubles the charge.
  • Wrong calculation period — interest accrued over an incorrect period or after settlement.
  • Failure to credit payments — payments that were made but not deducted from the principal before the interest was calculated.

How do you check and get a refund?

  1. Obtain a full case statement including the breakdown of principal, interest, linkage, and expenses.
  2. Check the interest and linkage rates applied against what the law permits, and check the period.
  3. File the appropriate application (including a claim of payment, "pareti") to correct the calculation and recover amounts collected in excess.
