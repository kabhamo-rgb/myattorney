How Do I Know If I Was Over-Charged in Enforcement Proceedings?

Many people keep paying in enforcement proceedings even after their actual debt has been covered — or have funds garnished that may not be garnished at all. In this guide we explain when over-collection arises, which funds are protected, how to check for yourself, and how to file a refund request.

In brief: If the amount collected from you in enforcement proceedings is higher than the debt that actually existed — you may be entitled to a refund. You can check this for free, and the main legal procedure for it is a claim of payment ("pareti").

What counts as "over-collection" or "over-garnishment"?

Over-collection is a situation in which the amount collected from you within your enforcement case exceeds the debt you actually owed. Over-garnishment is the imposition of a garnishment on assets or funds beyond what is required to cover the debt, or on funds that the law prohibits garnishing.

When does over-collection arise? Common cases

  • Interest or linkage calculated in error — an incorrect calculation of default interest or linkage that inflates the debt.
  • Double charging — the same debt component collected twice, or payments made that were not deducted from the balance.
  • Collection after the debt was cleared — continued collection or garnishment even after the debt was paid in full.
  • Failure to record direct payments — payments made directly to the creditor that were not reported to the case.
  • Garnishment of protected funds — garnishment of an account or salary beyond the amounts the law protects.

Which funds may not be garnished?

Israeli law protects certain amounts in order to ensure a dignified existence. Among other things, there is protection for part of the salary, for part of the funds in the bank account, and for certain allowances and pensions. The exact amounts are updated from time to time — so it is important to check the current figures in the official sources (see links below). If protected funds were garnished from you, you may be entitled to a refund and to cancellation of the garnishment.

How to check for yourself — step by step

  1. Obtain a case statement from the Enforcement and Collection Authority with a breakdown of the debt, interest, and payments.
  2. Compare the original debt balance against all amounts actually collected and paid.
  3. Check the interest and linkage components — whether they were calculated lawfully.
  4. Locate payments that were not deducted or double charges.
  5. Check whether protected funds were garnished (salary/allowances/protected amount in the account).

What to do if you discovered over-collection?

The main procedure is filing a claim of payment ("pareti") under Section 19 of the Execution Law. In this request the debtor argues that the debt was paid — in full or in part — and asks the court to order the return of amounts collected beyond the debt and to stop further collection. In appropriate cases, one may also request cancellation of the garnishment on protected funds and their return.

The procedure requires precision in presenting the documents and calculations, so it is advisable to seek the help of an attorney who specializes in enforcement proceedings.
